REQ-DISP-0NN-A  (display polarity, user decision on #251)   -- unchanged from revision 1
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The system SHALL decide the display polarity at the Presentation LUT stage and nowhere earlier.

  (a) WHEN `xpe_apply_presentation_lut` is called, the system SHALL read the 1024-entry table backwards:
      `output[i] = lutData[1023 - index]`, with `index = clamp(round(input[i] * 1023), 0, 1023)` (REQ-DISP-020) and the input
      clamped to [0, 1] first (REQ-DISP-021). The highest input value is therefore shown dark.
  (b) WHEN `xpe_apply_presentation_lut_ex` is called with `XPE_PRESENTATION_INVERTED`, the system SHALL behave as in (a);
      with `XPE_PRESENTATION_AS_IS` it SHALL apply `output[i] = lutData[index]` (the mapping of REQ-DISP-019 as it stood before
      this requirement).
  (c) IF `polarity` is neither value, THEN `xpe_apply_presentation_lut_ex` SHALL return `XPE_ERR_INVALID_INPUT` and leave the
      image untouched.
  (d) The values of every stage before the Presentation LUT SHALL keep their meaning ("a larger value is brighter" in the
      MONOCHROME2 sense carried from the reader); `xpe_dicom_write` SHALL keep writing MONOCHROME2 with Presentation LUT Shape
      IDENTITY, the pixel data already carrying the inversion.

Rationale, amendment of REQ-DISP-019, tests, falsification, cost and CALLER RESPONSIBILITY: as in revision 1 (the MONOCHROME1
polarity contract is deferred to the DICOM stage by the lead).

REQ-DISP-0NN-B  (anatomy-based automatic VOI window, user decision on #251)   -- REVISED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHEN `xpe_voi_auto_window` is called with a valid float32 image and a non-NULL output, the system SHALL fill `outParams` with a
VOI window derived from the anatomy in the image:

  (a) [revised] Build a histogram of 1024 bins between the lowest and the highest value of the pixels it is built from.
      An image of at least 2^20 pixels (kAutoWindowFullPassPixelLimit) is histogrammed from every Ns-th pixel of every Ns-th row
      (Ns = kAutoWindowSampleStride = 4); a smaller image is histogrammed from every pixel. A sample SHALL be used only if it
      spans a value range and the pixels OUTSIDE that value range are at most 0.2 % of the image (kAutoWindowMaxUnsampledMass =
      0.002); otherwise the system SHALL histogram every pixel of the image.
  (b) Split the histogram into two classes with Otsu's method. The HIGH class is the background (unattenuated beam), the low
      class is the anatomy.
  (c) Window low = the 0.5 % quantile of the anatomy class (kAutoWindowAnatomyLowQuantile = 0.005);
      window high = the 5 % quantile of the background class (kAutoWindowBackgroundLowQuantile = 0.05).
  (d) Output: mode `XPE_VOI_LINEAR_EXACT`, center = (low + high) / 2, width = high - low (at least one histogram bin),
      minOut 0.0, maxOut 1.0.
  (e) [revised] IF either class holds under 2 % of the histogrammed pixels (kAutoWindowMinClassFraction = 0.02), OR the Otsu
      separability (between-class variance / total variance) is under 0.75 (kAutoWindowMinSeparability = 0.75), THEN the window
      SHALL be the 1 % .. 99 % quantile range of the histogram of EVERY pixel of the image (to the resolution of one bin, 0.1 %
      of the value range) -- a fallback reached from a sample SHALL be recomputed from every pixel before it is used -- and ONE
      Info alert "VOI auto window: <reason> -- the whole-image 1%..99% window was used instead of an anatomy window" SHALL be
      posted.
  (f) IF the image is flat (maximum == minimum) THEN center = that value, width = 1.0, with the same Info alert.
  (g) [revised] IF `img` or `outParams` is NULL, `img->data` is NULL, the image is empty or its dataSize is inconsistent, THEN
      `XPE_ERR_INVALID_INPUT`; IF `img->format` is not float32, THEN `XPE_ERR_UNSUPPORTED_FORMAT`; IF any pixel is NaN or
      infinite, THEN `XPE_ERR_INVALID_INPUT`; IF the window cannot be held as a float -- its center or width is beyond FLT_MAX
      in magnitude (two finite classes at -FLT_MAX and +FLT_MAX span 2 * FLT_MAX) or its width is not above zero as a float --
      THEN `XPE_ERR_INVALID_INPUT`. In every error case `outParams` SHALL be untouched. On `XPE_OK` the center and the width
      SHALL be finite floats and the width SHALL be positive.
  (h) The window SHALL be derived from the values that reach the VOI stage, in whatever domain they are in.
  (i) [new] The user-visible behaviour of (a) is a statistical guarantee on a REPRESENTATIVE sample and an exact one on
      everything else: the pixels the sample cannot see are bounded by 0.2 % of the image, and above that bound the whole image
      is read. (The 0.2 % lies under the lowest quantile the method reads, 0.5 %.)

Proposed performance requirement (the lead decides; no budget existed): <= 16 ms for 3072 x 3072, the interactive budget of
REQ-DISP-016. Measured (local, shared machine, 21 repetitions, QA-B-214b): about 3.8 ms for a real frame (sample trusted),
about 11 ms when the whole image has to be histogrammed, 1.2 ms for 1000 x 1000. [revision 1 said 2.8-3.0 ms: the unsampled
count added about 1 ms; it was 4.7 ms before it was merged into the single full pass.]

Limits to state in the requirement note: (1) the background is assumed to be the HIGH end of the data (for background-low data
the window would isolate the wrong class; a future parameter could name the side); (2) a collimator shadow (darker than the
anatomy) is counted into the anatomy class and widens the window; (3) an image that is almost entirely anatomy with two tissue
populations (a hand filling the frame) is split into "anatomy" and "background" among its own tissues; (4) constants were chosen
on one real wrist frame -- QA-B-214 report section 2 and QA-B-214b report section 1 give the measurements; (5) a sample that
the mass test trusts can still carry less than 0.2 % of unseen pixels: the quantiles move by at most that mass.
